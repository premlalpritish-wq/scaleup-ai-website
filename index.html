01 The problem

Published science is not executable. Turning it into a model is still a manual engineering task.

Process simulators can represent reaction kinetics, thermodynamics, mass and energy balances and transport when the right models and inputs are supplied. The hard part is supplying them: the knowledge that determines which model applies, with which parameters, over which domain — and whether the answer holds.

01Fragmented knowledge

The relevant science is scattered across research papers, equations, experimental results and models that already exist. No single place holds the equations, the conditions, the measured values and the assumptions together.

02Manual model construction

Turning that knowledge into a working computational model means choosing a formulation, parameterising it, implementing it and checking it. Each step is expert work, done again per process.

03Domain of applicability

A model is only meaningful inside a domain of applicability. Its assumptions, fitted parameters and known limitations decide where it may be trusted and where it may not.

04Composition is not automatic

Individual models do not add up to a valid larger simulation. Interfaces, units, assumptions and interactions between coupled components have to be evaluated before they can be connected.

05Scale needs more than numbers

Moving a laboratory process towards larger scale needs more than extracted values. Conditions that hold in a small vessel may not hold in a large one, and the governing constraints change.

What ScaleUp AI is working towards

Making that path more systematic and computationally accessible: reading scientific knowledge into structured form, turning it into executable models, checking those models against experimental observation, and eventually connecting compatible ones. The first stage — structured, evidence-grounded extraction — is built and demonstrable on this page. The rest is the research programme, not a description of current software.

TECHNICAL LOGIC Why the structured layer comes first. A model can only be generated from knowledge that is specific enough to implement. That means parameters with units and conditions, relationships between process stages, and a recorded source for each value — which is what the extraction stage produces. Extracting a process description is not the same as executing a process model: the first is a record, the second is code that runs and can disagree with reality. Only the record exists here today.

Proposed workflow

How one paper becomes an evaluated model

Scientific modelling is iterative, not a one-pass extraction. A candidate model is executed, compared against the experimental observations the paper reports, and either recorded or revised. A revision is a change to an assumption, a parameter, an equation or a piece of missing information that is traceable to evidence — not an arbitrary edit made to reach an answer already decided.

03 Proposed architecture

Many sources, many models, one shared engine

A single paper is not enough to simulate a complex chemical or biological process. Different sources describe different phenomena, mechanisms and operating conditions. The engine's long-term purpose is to build each model separately, test it against its own evidence, and then connect the ones that are genuinely compatible inside one shared simulation.

Coverage

From individual models to system coverage

Coverage grows as compatible models are added, but coverage and accuracy are different things. A wider system describes more of the process; whether it predicts better depends on the quality of each model, their compatibility, the data behind them, the assumptions they carry, and how the coupled result is validated.

01

Individual models

Separate models each describe one specific phenomenon, built and evaluated against their own evidence.

02

Coupled models

Compatible models exchange the quantities they share and represent phenomena that interact rather than in isolation.

03

Integrated process simulation

The composed system represents a larger portion of the process, subject to validation of the combined behaviour.

BOUND Adding more models does not automatically make predictions more accurate. A wider composed system can be worse than a narrower one if a component is poorly specified, incompatible with the others, or wrong in a way the others inherit. These stages describe intended system coverage, not a benchmark, and not a claim that accuracy increases with each step.

B What the system understands

What the system understands

Scientific knowledge is fragmented across papers, equations, experimental results, and specialist communities. Reusing it computationally requires more than summarising papers: relevant equations, variables, parameters, units, assumptions, evidence, and constraints must be extracted and represented in a structured form.

Extract

Equations, variables, parameters, and conditions must be pulled out of prose, tables, and figures — each with its unit and its source.

Provenance

Reported facts and inferred quantities must stay distinct, so a value can be traced to the page and excerpt it came from.

Assumptions

Assumptions and uncertainty must be represented explicitly, not smoothed over to make the record look complete.

Units

Units and model interfaces must be reconciled before separate models can be composed and executed together.

Applicability

Whether evidence and models apply to the same system must be evaluated — a difference in strain or condition is a compatibility question, not an automatic disqualification.

Composition

Compatible models must be composed into larger simulations without losing their evidence, assumptions, or limitations.

THESIS Scientific knowledge must become computationally reusable without losing its evidence, assumptions, or limitations. That is the central message of ScaleUp AI: structure first, so models can be built, checked, and composed on top of something trustworthy.

        09 Uncertainty & missing information
        
        
        
          
        The system does not fill in what the paper left out.
        
          
        
            These are three distinct, separately stored lists in
            ProcessState. A field that is absent is
            reported as absent, not defaulted.
          
        
        
        
        
        

        
        
          WHY
          
            Discipline over completeness. A schema that always fills every
            field will always look more useful than one that admits a gap. For
            process development, a confidently wrong setpoint is worse than a
            recorded absence — so the absence is part of the output.
          
        
        
      
        
    
        

    
    
        
      
        
        
        C A concrete example of scientific reasoning
        
        
        
          
        A concrete example of scientific reasoning
        
          
        
            The prototype on this page is built from a real paper about
            recombinant human insulin production in E. coli.
            It exposes missing dissolved-oxygen information, and shows how a
            potentially relevant finding from a different strain can be inspected
            without being automatically treated as transferable.
          
        
        
        
        
        
          
        
            
        What the model exposes
        
            
        
              The paper reports agitation, aeration, dissolved-oxygen control,
              pressure, and exact feed profiles only partially — or not at all.
              The model records these as missing information rather than filling
              them in, so an engineer can see exactly what is unknown before
              attempting any prediction.
            
        
          
        
          
        
            
        What a strain difference means
        
            
        
              A finding reported for a different E. coli
              strain does not by itself prove evidence invalid. It introduces a
              compatibility question — same organism, different background — that
              must be evaluated before the evidence is reused.
            
        
          
        
          
        
            
        Why this matters
        
            
        
              Preserving evidence and surfacing unresolved questions is the value:
              the system reports what it does not know, so a human can decide
              whether a cross-strain result applies, instead of silently
              adopting it.
