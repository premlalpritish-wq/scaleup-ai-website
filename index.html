01 The problem

The process is in the paper. The process is not in the data.

A published bioprocess is split across methods, tables, figures and supplementary text. Reading one parameter out of it is easy. Getting a faithful, machine-readable picture of the whole process is not.

01Scattered

Operating conditions live in methods prose, mass balances in tables, and the sequence of operations in a figure. No single field holds the process.

02Interdependent

A parameter is only meaningful with its unit, its stage, and the operation it belongs to. A bare number loses all three.

03Incompletely reported

Papers omit setpoints, inducer identity, agitation and aeration. The gaps are real, and a value-filled schema would hide them.

04Internally inconsistent

The same quantity can be stated one way in Methods and another in Results. Flattening that to a single value destroys the conflict.

Useful process knowledge has to carry five things at once

parameters relationships evidence provenance uncertainty missing information

        Stable identity
        
        
        
          Nodes are keyed by a scoped, content-derived ID rather than a display
          label or an array position, so the same operation keeps the same
          identity across runs. Reconciliation assigns these canonical IDs from a
          source-supported occurrence key.
        
        
        
        
      
        
    
        

    
    
        
      
        
        
        06 Uncertainty & missing information
        
        
        
          
        The system does not fill in what the paper left out.
        
          
        
            These are three distinct, separately stored lists in
            ProcessState. A field that is absent is
            reported as absent, not defaulted.
          
        
        
        
        
        

        
        
          WHY
          
            Discipline over completeness. A schema that always fills every
            field will always look more useful than one that admits a gap. For
            process development, a confidently wrong setpoint is worse than a
            recorded absence — so the absence is part of the output.
          
        
        
      
        
    
        

    
    
        
      
        
        
        07 Validation record
        
        
        
          
        Where the system rejects the model
        
          
        
            A structured schema is easy to generate and easy to get subtly wrong.
            The interesting question is not whether the pipeline produces output,
            but what it refuses. These are the recorded results of repeated live
            extraction runs against this paper, including the failures.
          
        
        
        

        
        

        
        
          LIMIT

          BOUND
          
            Not built. No process simulation, autonomous optimisation,
            industrial scale-up, experimental planning, closed-loop operation or
            process-development automation exists in the codebase today.
            ModelResult and
            Recommendation are reserved fields on the
            schema and are empty in every run shown here. Treat steps 02–05 as
            intent, not capability.
