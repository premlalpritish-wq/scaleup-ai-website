A The problem

Scientific knowledge is abundant. Turning it into working models is still hard.

This is the founder's firsthand experience, and a hypothesis about a broader engineering problem. It is not a claim that every engineer faces the same workflow.

When we tried to model recombinant human insulin production using E. coli, the work looked like this:

  1. Finding relevant scientific papers.
  2. Extracting experimental conditions, parameters, and equations.
  3. Translating scientific information into executable code.
  4. Building individual process models.
  5. Connecting the models into a simulation.

The science already existed in the literature. The hard part was turning it into reusable, executable models. ScaleUp AI is building towards a future where that translation is automated.

C From scientific literature to executable models

From scientific literature to executable models

The demonstration below is captured from the ScaleUp AI Godot prototype: a desktop application that composes scientific process models through typed ports and runs them in dependency order, with a 3D evidence replay of the Zieliński et al. (2019) recombinant human insulin process.

Compose models

Registered models (Zielinski insulin process, Ganjave, Morris material balance) are added from a palette and connected through typed, unit-aware ports. Required inputs have no defaults: a missing value blocks execution.

Run and validate

A graph can be validated into an execution plan, or executed. Cycles are rejected, and each model runs in its own interpreter subprocess because their packages cannot coexist.

Replay evidence

The 3D replay restates numbers read from a published paper and the accounting identities it supports. It reports no growth kinetics, no oxygen-transfer data, no AU calibration and no purification model - and declares those absences explicitly.

Investigate gaps

Under each missing quantity an Investigate button opens a panel: the model and run it was checked against, the gap in the model's own words, candidate findings from the literature with verbatim quoted passages, a dimension-by-dimension condition comparison, a verdict (incompatible / needs checking / compatible but not yet validated), and ten checkable validation criteria.

Check compatibility

A model registry records every model with a stable id, version, digests, ports and units. A compatibility engine assesses whether two models may be composed, with refused pairs reporting the specific difference and what would resolve it.

Preserve provenance

Every displayed value carries a plain-language provenance label: stated in the source, read from a published figure, calculated from the available data, or not established by the available evidence.

STATUS What this is and is not. The screenshot above is captured from the running web build of the simulator (Godot 4.7, Web export), which is now embedded and runnable in the browser. The insulin model is an evidence replay, not a predictive simulator - it restates published values and refuses to invent missing ones.

D Technical foundations

What makes prompt-to-simulation possible

Five foundations underpin the product. They are the building blocks, not proof that the entire automated workflow is complete.

Literature extraction

Turn scientific papers into structured process information: equations, variables, parameters, units, conditions, and sources.

Evidence and provenance

Distinguish reported facts, calculations, inferences, assumptions, and unknowns, so every value can be traced to its source.

Executable models

Represent scientific relationships and process behaviour computationally, with units and interfaces declared.

Model composition

Connect compatible models through declared interfaces, units, and dependencies; refuse incompatible connections and report why.

Simulation and investigation

Execute models, inspect results, and identify gaps that need further evidence or validation.

THESIS Scientific knowledge must become computationally reusable without losing its evidence, assumptions, or limitations.

E A concrete proof of concept

Why start with insulin production

ScaleUp AI began with a concrete engineering goal: modelling recombinant human insulin production using E. coli. This is the first concrete biological engineering problem used to build and test the approach. Scientific papers were researched and retrieved using AI-assisted research; relevant information was extracted and encoded into computational models; models were connected and executed through a composition system; and a Godot environment was built to inspect and visualise the resulting model and evidence. This is the manual version of the workflow ScaleUp AI aims to automate.

What the model exposes

The paper reports agitation, aeration, dissolved-oxygen control, pressure, and exact feed profiles only partially - or not at all. The model records these as missing information rather than filling them in, so an engineer can see exactly what is unknown before attempting any prediction.

What a strain difference means

A finding reported for a different E. coli strain does not by itself prove evidence invalid. It introduces a compatibility question - same organism, different background - that must be evaluated before the evidence is reused.

Why this matters

Preserving evidence and surfacing unresolved questions is the value: the system reports what it does not know, so a human can decide whether a cross-strain result applies, instead of silently adopting it.

G Current versus future

What exists today and what is being built

The aspiration is ambitious; descriptions of current functionality are precise. Three levels, clearly separated.

Demonstrated today

  • A manually assembled literature-to-model workflow.
  • Structured scientific process information with traceable evidence and provenance.
  • An executable model composition pipeline with typed, unit-aware ports.
  • An interactive simulator and evidence replay for the insulin process.

Being developed

  • More reliable automated extraction and model construction.
  • Automated model discovery, compatibility assessment, and composition.
  • A progressively automated path from a natural-language goal to a runnable simulation.

Long-term vision

  • Describe almost any physical thing or process in natural language.
  • Have AI discover the relevant science and engineering knowledge.
  • Automatically construct and assemble the necessary computational models.
  • Generate an interactive simulation to explore how the physical idea could work.
HONESTY The insulin replay is evidence, not a prediction. It restates published values and reports its own gaps. It does not reliably simulate arbitrary physical systems, automatically research and construct every model end-to-end, predict real-world outcomes accurately without validation, or produce manufacturing-ready designs. The Ganjave model and other registered models are not validated for predictive use.
