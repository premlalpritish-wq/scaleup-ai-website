Four Steps from Paper to Playable Simulation

How natural-language scientific protocols compile into mass-conserved, interactive 3D digital twins.

STEP 01 // MAP LAB SETUP

1. Map the Lab Setup

Automatically turns the protocol into a 2D equipment diagram (tanks, centrifuges, pipes) and checks what goes in and out of every step.

{ "step": "Map_Equipment", "tanks": 6, "flow_balance": "CONSERVED", "blueprint": "P&ID_SVG" }
STEP 02 // LOCK NUMBERS

2. Lock in Real Paper Numbers

Pulls verified equations, temperatures, and yields directly from our scientific library—every number links back to the exact sentence in the source paper.

{ "doi": "10.1016/j.algal.2024", "sha256": "8f3b2a19e...", "verified_quote": "e912c4...", "status": "DIRECT_EVIDENCE" }
STEP 03 // SEARCH GAPS

3. Auto-Search or Stop Safely

If a number is missing, our research agent searches published papers to find it. If it has not been published, ScaleUp stops and flags the gap instead of hallucinating.

raise InsufficientEvidenceError( missing_param="dissolution_rate", action="SEARCHING_OPENALEX", status="HALTED_NO_GUESSING" )
STEP 04 // 3D PLAYGROUND

4. Watch & Test It in 3D

Launches an interactive 3D lab bench in Godot 4 where you can watch the batch flow from tank to tank, scrub through time, and tweak settings to see what breaks.

Godot4_Simulation: active_vessels: 6 particle_tracking: 120 mass_conservation: 100% framerate: 60 FPS

[PORT TYPING]

Compose Models

Registered models (Zielinski insulin process, Ganjave, Morris material balance) are connected through typed, unit-aware ports. Required inputs have no defaults: a missing value blocks execution.

[VALIDATION]

Run and Validate

A graph can be validated into an execution plan or executed. Cycles are rejected, and each model runs in its own interpreter subprocess because dependencies cannot co-exist.

[FAIL-CLOSED]

Replay Evidence

The 3D replay restates numbers read from a published paper and the accounting identities it supports. It reports no growth kinetics, no oxygen-transfer data, no AU calibration and no purification model - and declares those absences explicitly.

[AUTONOMOUS]

Investigate Gaps

Under each missing quantity an Investigate button opens a panel: the model and run it was checked against, the gap in the model's own words, candidate findings from the literature with verbatim quoted passages, a dimension-by-dimension condition comparison, a verdict (incompatible / needs checking / compatible but not yet validated), and ten checkable validation criteria.

[STRAIN BRIDGE]

Check Compatibility

A model registry records every model with a stable id, version, digests, ports and units. A compatibility engine assesses whether two models may be composed, with refused pairs reporting the specific difference and what would resolve it.

[PROVENANCE]

Preserve Provenance

Every displayed value carries a plain-language provenance label: stated in the source, read from a published figure, calculated from the available data, or not established by the available evidence.

[SCIENTIFIC PROOF]

Why Dissolved Oxygen & Strain Compatibility Matter

How ScaleUp tracks bacterial backgrounds and physical aeration limits so you never scale up on bad assumptions.

[TELEMETRY]

What the Model Exposes

The paper reports agitation, aeration, dissolved-oxygen control, pressure, and exact feed profiles only partially - or not at all. The model records these as missing information rather than filling them in, so an engineer can see exactly what is unknown before attempting any prediction.

[STRAIN BOUNDARY]

What a Strain Difference Means

A finding reported for a different E. coli strain does not by itself prove evidence invalid. It introduces a compatibility question - same organism, different background - that must be evaluated before the evidence is reused or transferred.

[INTEGRITY]

Why This Matters

Preserving evidence and surfacing unresolved questions is the value: the system reports what it does not know, so a human can decide whether a cross-strain result applies, instead of silently adopting it.
